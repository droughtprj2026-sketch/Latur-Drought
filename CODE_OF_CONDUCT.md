# Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We — the maintainers and contributors of the **Real-Time Drought
Monitoring platform for Latur District, Marathwada** — pledge to make
participation in our project a harassment-free, respectful, and
collaborative experience for everyone.

The project exists to make scientific drought information more
accessible and actionable. We expect everyone contributing to it —
developers, data scientists, GIS analysts, students, reviewers, and
end users — to behave in a way that supports that mission.

This Code applies to code contributions, issue discussions, pull
request reviews, documentation, data uploads, meetings, and any public
or private communication conducted on behalf of the project.

---

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment:

- Using welcoming and inclusive language.
- Being respectful of differing viewpoints, backgrounds, and experiences.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback on code, data,
  and scientific methodology.
- Showing empathy toward other community members, especially newcomers
  and students.
- Attributing data sources, prior work, and third-party scripts
  correctly (CHIRPS, Sentinel-2 / Copernicus, Google Earth Engine,
  IMD, Open-Meteo, etc.).
- Focusing on what is best for the project and the communities it is
  built to serve — especially the farmers, administrators, and
  researchers in the Marathwada region.

Examples of unacceptable behavior:

- Harassment, insults, or derogatory comments — public or private.
- Personal or political attacks on contributors.
- Discrimination on the basis of gender, caste, religion, sexual
  orientation, disability, nationality, age, or any other protected
  characteristic.
- Publishing someone's private information (address, phone number,
  personal email) without explicit permission.
- Sexualized language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Submitting data or results known to be fabricated, falsified, or
  deliberately misleading.
- Removing attribution from upstream datasets or presenting
  third-party scientific work as one's own.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, issue trackers, or review
  processes.

---

## Scientific Integrity

Because this project publishes derived drought indicators (SPI, NDVI,
VHI, PCI, EVI, CDI) that may inform real decisions about agriculture,
water allocation, and emergency response, contributors are additionally
expected to:

- Clearly document the methods and parameters used for any index
  computation (e.g. SPI calibration window, CDI weights, cloud-mask
  thresholds).
- Flag uncertainty — e.g. the current ~10-year CHIRPS record is shorter
  than the 30 years WMO recommends for SPI, and extreme values should
  be interpreted with care.
- Not overstate the accuracy or operational readiness of outputs.
- Keep raw and processed data traceable: do not silently overwrite
  processed outputs in `data/processed/` without updating the manifest.
- Disclose conflicts of interest when they exist.

## Our Responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying the standards of
acceptable behavior and are expected to take appropriate and fair
corrective action in response to any behavior they deem inappropriate,
threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or
reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, data uploads, and
other contributions that are not aligned with this Code of Conduct, and
to ban, temporarily or permanently, any contributor for behavior they
deem inappropriate.

---

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces, and also
applies when an individual is representing the project in public
spaces. Examples include using an official project e-mail address,
posting via an official social media account, or acting as an
appointed representative at an online or offline event (academic
conferences, poster sessions, demos to government bodies, etc.).

- Names (real, usernames, or pseudonyms) of any individuals involved.
- A description of what happened and, if possible, links or

- Any additional context you believe may be helpful.

project maintainers are obligated to maintain confidentiality with
regard to the reporter of an incident.

---

## Enforcement

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce the Code of Conduct in good
faith may face temporary or permanent repercussions as determined by
other project leadership.

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior
may be addressed by one or more of the following, depending on
severity:

1. **Correction** — a private, written clarification from a maintainer
   explaining what was wrong and why.
2. **Warning** — a formal warning with consequences for continued
   behavior. No interaction with the people involved for a specified
   period.
3. **Temporary ban** — a temporary ban from any interaction or public
   communication with the community for a specified period.
4. **Permanent ban** — a permanent ban from any sort of public
   interaction within the community.
